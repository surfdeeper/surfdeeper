Finding trim is the foundation of easy paddling, early entry, and clean takeoffs. It’s about letting the board glide with minimal drag — not forcing the nose up or pushing it down.

## The Two Big Cues

- Longboard (beginner-friendly): keep the nose close to the water — about an inch above or just feathering the surface. If the nose is way up, you’re too far back and plowing; if it’s starting to bury, you’re too far forward.
- Shortboard: think neutral, flat, planing. Keep a light sternum lift so the board runs level; don’t try to visibly hold the nose up — the rocker and speed take care of clearance.

## Micro-Adjust, Don’t Over-Correct

- Slide forward/back in inches, not feet, to keep glide without stalling or pearling
- Use a touch of cobra (sternum lift) to shift pressure back on longboards without losing position
- On shortboards, prioritize early speed and a set angle down the line over lifting the nose

## Common Mistakes

- Chasing a big visible nose lift (both board types): adds drag and kills glide
- Pressing the chest down (longboards): sinks the nose and plows
- Scooting too late (any board): reactive fixes that cost angle and speed

## Practice Cues

- Film 10–15 seconds of paddling; look for level trim and a quiet lower body
- In small waves, adjust your position a few inches at a time and feel for less splash and more glide
- Pair trim drills with [Paddling Posture](:paddling-posture) and [Efficient Paddling](:paddling-efficiency)

## Plowing vs. Planing vs. Pearling

- Planing (goal): board feels light and fast with minimal splash; water releases cleanly off the rails and tail.
- Plowing (too far back/too much drag): nose rides high, bow wave/splash off the chest area, wake feels sticky. Fix: slide forward a few inches and/or relax legs; seek a level, quiet glide.
- Pearling (too far forward/late): nose starts to bite or go under as the wave lifts. Fix: add a touch of cobra or slide back an inch or two; angle down the line and keep paddling for speed.
